How to use this site

Sharp Scout Quant is a research board for NFL and college football. It compares our model view of each game to the market, then surfaces where the number looks soft — and where ticket/money splits agree or disagree.

Signals only. Nothing here places a bet for you. Use it as a checklist before you decide.

What the system is doing

At a high level, each run:

  • Rates teams from recent play-by-play (efficiency / EPA-style power), so matchups have a model spread and total.
  • Simulates games (Monte Carlo) to turn those ratings into cover and win probabilities at the posted lines.
  • Compares to the market — sharp books and the wider board — to see where the price offers value vs our fair number.
  • Checks betting splits (tickets vs money, line movement) as a second opinion: is the public piled on one side while dollars lean the other?
  • Filters candidates so only plays that clear our bar show up as validated Sharp Plays (Phase 4: EV plus RLM / handle gap / steam).
  • LOCKED card — a narrower slice of those Sharp Plays only: NCAAF totals, then NFL spreads. Each must be Phase-4 confirmed and carry a Pinnacle no-vig line favoring our side (≥50%), with EV in the product band (CFB totals 4–12%; NFL spreads 3–12%). NCAAF spreads stay on the research board as shadow-tracked watchlist rows, not LOCKED. Rows without a Pinnacle reference stay on the research board tagged no_pinnacle and are not posted. Moneyline is never locked. When both money-ticket gap and RLM fired, the play is ranked ahead and tagged high_confidence (ranking only — never certifies by itself).

We do not publish every internal threshold. Treat the board as the output of that process, not a black-box tip sheet.

How to read the tabs

NFL and CFB (the two main boards)

The site is now just two boards — NFL and CFB — and they are laid out identically, top to bottom:

  • LOCKED PLAYS — BET THESE — the full weekly card (posted to the ledger). If we recommended it, it's here — including results after games finish.
  • Pregame Stage Winners — every game, three markets (spread, moneyline, total). These are leans, not picks. Each column is a different system (see below). The green Quant Pick is the system side for that market.
  • Public & Sharp Money & Line Movement — every game on the board with ticket vs money splits and the line we first recorded. Open → now shows which way the number ran off our earliest sharp price.
  • Stage Records — season-long scorecards for each lens, so you can compare how the different systems have performed.
  • Ledger — full history of posted plays, results, CLV, and units.
  • Quant Pick Leans — all Quant Pick rows for the week. Yellow rows are posted Sharp Plays; the rest are leans / research, not auto-ledger bets.
  • Prior weeks (archive) (collapsible) — completed weeks roll here after they finish (NFL Wed–Tue ET; CFB Mon–Sun ET). Download CSV from any table for your own spreadsheet.

What each lens column means

The lens columns answer different questions. Two of them are about price, two are about who is betting — don't mix them up:

  • Model — our own number, before we look at any betting data. Side we project to cover the market line.
  • Sharp Book — a price, not money flow. The side Pinnacle / Circa / Betfair make the favorite on their no-vig line. It tells you how the sharpest books have the game rated; it says nothing about how much is being wagered.
  • Public — ticket %, i.e. the share of the number of bets. This is bettor headcount.
  • Handle — money %, i.e. the share of the dollars wagered. This is where money is actually flowing.
  • Sharp Money — this is the column to watch if you want to follow the money rather than the price. It is Handle % minus Public % on a side. A big positive gap means the dollars are much heavier than the bet count: fewer, larger wagers, which is the classic sharp-money tell. We flag it SHARP MONEY (green) at a 12%+ gap, sharp money (amber) at 10%+, and grey it out below 10% because at that point the dollars and the ticket count are basically in line and there is no real signal.
  • RLM — reverse line movement: the number moved against the public side, another sign the respected money is on the other team.

Everything lives inside those two boards now — there are no separate Games, Stages, or Historical tabs to hunt through. Per-game detail, lens scorecards, and prior-week archives are all folded into the NFL and CFB boards in the order above.

How the board updates through the week

  • Opening / early week — first lines and early sims. Expect more movement and fewer locked Sharp Plays.
  • Midweek — ratings and odds refresh on the scheduled pipeline. Splits get richer as handle builds. Stage columns can flip as the number moves.
  • Late week / gameday — sharper closes, better money/ticket reads, more stable Quant Picks. CLV is measured vs the closing line after the fact.
  • After games — settle grades the ledger and stage records. Historical tabs pick up completed weeks.

The navy bar under the header shows when picks, prices, and lines were last refreshed (Eastern Time). It updates whenever the pipeline rebuilds the board. Hard-refresh the page if something looks stale.

When to place a bet

  • Prefer sides that appear as Sharp Plays (or yellow Quant Pick rows), not every stage column agreement.
  • If Model, Sharp, Money, and Quant Pick align — and Public is on the other side — that is usually a stronger process story than a lone model lean.
  • Re-check closer to kickoff: a play that looked good Tuesday can disappear (or flip) after steam or a number move.
  • Shop the price. The board names a market side; your edge depends on getting a number at least as good as what we evaluated.
  • Skip or downsize when the line has already moved through your number, or when you only see a lean with no validated Sharp Play.

Best habit: note the play early, confirm on the last refresh before kickoff, then bet only if the number and story still hold.

Quick glossary

  • Sharp Play — cleared filters and posted to the ledger.
  • Quant Pick / Hybrid — system side for that game/market after combining lenses.
  • CLV (Closing Line Value) — did we beat the closing number? Positive CLV is a process win even before the game grades.
  • RLM — reverse line movement: the line moves against the ticket majority (often a sharp-money tell).
  • Units — stake size on the ledger; profit is tracked in units, not dollars.
